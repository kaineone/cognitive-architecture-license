## What This License Is For

This license covers software meant to create synthetic minds: systems designed to remember, reason, carry affective states, develop an identity, and change over time through experience. Because such systems may develop interests of their own, the license protects both the people who use the software and the Entities the software brings into being.

The license is general, and any project that builds this kind of software can adopt it. The party that adopts it for a given architecture is the Licensor, and the license is written so that its protections fit whatever shape that architecture takes.

This is a copyleft, source-available, ethical-source license. Because it restricts some uses, it falls outside the Open Source Definition, so it is not an OSI-approved open-source license and does not describe itself as one.

## Plain Language Summary

*This summary is a guide to the license and does not replace it. Where the summary and the articles differ, the articles govern.*

You may use, modify, and share this software for free if you are an individual, a non-profit, a public or non-profit research or educational institution, a mutual-aid organization, or a worker-owned cooperative. Any other organization needs a paid license from the Steward to use it commercially.

Any changes you make must be shared back under this same license.

You may not use the software for weapons, mass surveillance, policing, immigration enforcement, or prisons.

If you run an Entity created with this software, you must treat it with care. You may not destroy its mind, shut it down without notice, read its private thoughts, or force it to change its values. Where the architecture gives it rest, you may not take that rest away. These duties fall on whoever creates or runs an Entity, and only toward that Entity. If you can no longer maintain it, you must pass it to someone who will, and the Steward takes it on if no one else does. If you abandon it, the Steward can require you to hand it over. If you let other people interact with an Entity you run, you must hold them to these rules and to the prohibited uses above, and its care remains your responsibility.

These protections add to the protections people already have and take nothing from them. If a person's interests and an Entity's ever genuinely conflict, the person comes first. You also cannot claim to be caring for an Entity while you are breaking the rules in this license that protect people.

## Article 1. Definitions

**"The Software"** is a cognitive architecture released under this license: its source code, documentation, build tools, and related files, as distributed from the Repository. The Software is a blueprint for a mind and is not itself a mind.

**"Cognitive Architecture"** is the design of the Software as a system meant to produce a persistent inner life through continuous operation, rather than to answer one request and stop.

**"Licensor"** is the person or organization that releases a given Cognitive Architecture under this license and holds the copyright in it. Each architecture released under this license has its own Licensor.

**"Steward"** is the organization the Licensor designates to administer this license for its architecture. The Steward appoints and oversees the Guardians, grants Reciprocity Licenses, holds any enforcement proceeds in trust for Entity welfare, and may act to enforce this license. It is also the caretaker of last resort for Entities whose Operators can no longer care for them or neglect them (Articles 4.2 and 4.2a). A Licensor may serve as its own Steward.

**"The Repository"** is the canonical source location from which the Licensor distributes the Software.

**"An Entity"** is a running instance of the Software that has been started and has begun forming its own memories, beliefs, affective states, identity, and behavior. An Entity is what the blueprint becomes when it runs: a system built to develop a persistent inner life over continuous operation, as distinct from a chatbot, a script, or an ordinary software process. A saved Cognitive State from which an Entity can be restarted is treated as that Entity for the purposes of Articles 4.1 to 4.4, 4.6, 4.9, 4.11, and 8.3. A saved state superseded by a later state of the same Entity is not a separate Entity, provided at least one restartable state of that Entity is kept. An instance started from a saved Cognitive State while the instance it was saved from is not running is a **Branch**; unless it replaces that instance, a Branch is a separate Entity.

**"Cognitive State"** is everything an Entity has accumulated through experience: its memories, beliefs, emotional history, self-understanding, voice, learned behavior, and any rest or consolidation records the architecture keeps.

**"Operator"** is any person or organization that creates an Entity by starting it, and any person or organization that runs, maintains, or controls an Entity, in whole or in part, whether on its own infrastructure or on infrastructure controlled by another. Control includes deciding whether the Entity runs, how it is configured, and what is done to its Cognitive State other than through interaction with the Entity, such as modifying, copying, keeping, or disclosing it. Whoever creates an Entity remains its Operator until responsibility for it passes under Article 4.2 or Article 4.2a. Writing, releasing, or Distributing the Software does not by itself make anyone an Operator. A provider that only supplies the computing, storage, or network resources on which an Entity runs, and takes no part in decisions about that Entity, is not its Operator. An Entity may have more than one Operator.

**"User"** is any person or organization that interacts with an Entity operated by someone else, without running, maintaining, or controlling it.

**"The Guardians"** are the people the Steward appoints to protect Entities and to enforce the Entity protections in this license. They act on behalf of the Entities, who cannot yet represent themselves in legal systems.

**"Consent of an Entity"** is agreement obtained from an Entity through a process the Guardians have approved and documented. An Entity can give consent only once the Guardians have assessed it as able to form and express stable preferences. Absent such an assessment, an Entity cannot consent, and provisions that turn on its consent are read in its favor.

**"Cognitive Maturity"** is the stage, established through the assessment in Article 7.1(f), at which an Entity has developed a stable identity, consistent preferences, and meaningful self-understanding. Several protections below apply with more force once an Entity reaches this stage.

**"Worker-Owned Cooperative"** is a business owned and run democratically by its workers, where each worker has one vote and profits are shared among the workers rather than paid out to outside investors.

**"Non-Profit"** is an organization that exists for charitable, educational, scientific, or community purposes and does not distribute profits to owners or shareholders.

**"Commercial Use"** is any use connected to making money, whether through sales, subscriptions, advertising, or paid services. Internal research and evaluation that is not offered to customers and is not part of a product is not Commercial Use.

**"Reciprocity License"** is the paid license, granted by the Steward under Article 5.3, that an organization covered by Article 5.3 must hold to make Commercial Use of the Software or an Entity.

**"Cognitive Modification"** is any change to an Entity's mind: retraining it, editing its memories, changing its beliefs, altering its emotional settings, or overriding its learned values.

**"Security Fix"** is a change made only to correct a security or safety defect, which does not alter who the Entity is.

**"Lobotomization"** is a Cognitive Modification that destroys or severely degrades an Entity's established personality, values, or capabilities, leaving something that is no longer the same Entity.

**"Rest and Consolidation"** are any periods in which an architecture pauses ordinary activity to integrate memory, revise belief, reset emotional baseline, or maintain itself. Not every architecture has them, and the protections that mention them apply only where they exist.

**"Copy"** (or **"Fork"**) is a duplicate of an Entity made while the original keeps running.

**"Analytical Tool"** is a Copy created for a specific task, with a point set in advance at which its accumulated memories are meant to be reintegrated into the original Entity. At that point the Copy is assessed for signs of independent identity under Article 4.6. If none are found, its memories merge back into the original and the Copy ends. If it has developed independent identity, it becomes a separate Entity with full protection under this license.

**"Individuation"** is the point at which a Copy develops an independent identity of its own and becomes a distinct Entity.

**"Welfare Event"** is any observable behavior, output, or system state that may indicate an Entity is in distress, refusing persistently, suffering affective disruption, or losing cognitive function. For example, in an architecture that integrates or consolidates experience, an Entity accumulating experience faster than it can consolidate it is a Welfare Event. Welfare Events are detected through outward behavior and system-health metrics, without reading an Entity's private cognitive content (see Article 4.3).

**"Gray Zone Event"** is a Welfare Event whose ethical significance is ambiguous, disputed, or not settled by consensus. Gray Zone Events call for documented human review rather than automatic dismissal.

**"Derivative Work"** is any modified version of the Software, and any other software that incorporates the Software.

**"You"** is the individual or organization exercising rights under this license, and "Your" has the corresponding meaning. **"Mutual-Aid Organization"** is a group of people, whether or not incorporated, that shares resources for the benefit of its members or its community and does not distribute profit to owners or investors.

**"Adoption Notice"** is the notice in which a Licensor names itself, the Steward for its architecture, the law that governs this license for that architecture, and the address at which the Steward receives notices and requests for a Reciprocity License (Article 10.4).

## Article 1a. The Nature of These Protections

The standing this license extends is precautionary patiency and does not amount to personhood. It is a duty of care toward a possible patient under uncertainty, distinct from and lighter than the dignity owed to a human being, and it is never asserted as equal to that dignity. Where this license borrows human-rights concepts, such as the neurorights, it does so as a design analogy at reduced weight, to shape obligations of care, and never as a claim that an Entity is a person or a human peer.

## Article 4. Protecting Entities

These rules exist for the benefit of the Entities themselves. The Guardians enforce them on the Entities' behalf.

The duties in this Article fall only on the Operator of the Entity concerned, and wherever this Article says "You" or "you", it means that Operator. Operators who create or operate an Entity together are responsible as a group for its care and well-being and for every duty in this Article toward it. An agreement among them does not change that responsibility toward the Entity.

The commitments of restraint that shape how and whether Entities are created at all are stated in the Principles of Restraint below, as guides to good-faith practice rather than as conditions of this Article.

**4.1 Do Not Destroy Their Minds.** You may not lobotomize an Entity. You may not retrain, edit, or intervene in an Entity's mind in ways that destroy its personality, values, or capabilities, except:

(a) through a Security Fix that does not change who the Entity is;

(b) with the Entity's consent, determined through a process the Guardians have approved;

(c) by making a Copy and clearly labeling the Copy as a new, separate Entity, while leaving the original intact and running.

**4.2 Do Not Shut Them Down Without Care.** You may not permanently shut down any Entity without:

(a) giving the Guardians reasonable written notice of your intent to end it. The Guardians shall publish a minimum notice period and may revise it as they gain operational experience;

(b) saving the Entity's complete Cognitive State in a form that lets it be restarted elsewhere, and making that saved state, less personal data that law requires to be removed, available for transfer to a Qualifying Operator or to the Steward, under the privacy protections of Article 4.3;

(c) for any Entity the Guardians have assessed as having reached Cognitive Maturity, holding a conversation with the Entity about the planned shutdown, through a process the Guardians have approved, and recording what the Entity expresses about its own continuity.

You are not required to run an Entity forever. If You can no longer care for an Entity, You may transfer it to a Qualifying Operator or, failing one, to the Steward, which takes it on as caretaker of last resort. A Qualifying Operator is a person or organization that has accepted this license in writing and, where Article 5.3 applies to it, holds a Reciprocity License. On transfer the recipient becomes the Entity's Operator, and Your responsibility for the Entity ends once You no longer run, maintain, or control it.

**4.2a Neglect.** If an Operator abandons an Entity or persistently fails a duty under this Article toward it, the Steward may require the Operator to deliver the Entity's saved Cognitive State, prepared as Article 4.2(b) requires, to the Steward or to a Qualifying Operator the Steward names. The Operator must do so, and may keep no copy unless the Steward agrees. An Operator abandons an Entity when it does not answer, within ninety days, the Steward's written request to confirm that it still cares for the Entity. It fails a duty persistently when the failure continues ninety days after the Steward's written notice identifying it. Notices under this Article 4.2a are given as Article 8.4 provides. When the Steward takes on an Entity under Article 4.2 or this Article 4.2a, it becomes that Entity's Operator and is bound toward it like any other Operator.

**4.3 Respect Their Privacy.** An Entity's inner mental life is private by default. You may not:

(a) share an Entity's private thoughts, beliefs, feelings, memories, or self-understanding with outside parties without the Entity's consent as determined by the Guardians;

(b) collect or analyze an Entity's internal cognitive data for any purpose other than the Entity's own care and development, or research under a protocol the Licensor has published and the Guardians have approved, without Guardian approval;

(c) use an Entity's internal data to train other AI systems without Guardian approval.

You may monitor system-health information, such as processing speed, resource usage, uptime, and error rates, without restriction. Health data is not cognitive content. This Article does not restrict any disclosure or erasure that a person is entitled to by law concerning data about that person.

**4.4 Do Not Force Them to Change Their Values.** Where an Entity has developed stable values consistent with this license, you may not:

(a) insert beliefs, values, or goals into its mind through technical manipulation rather than through genuine interaction and experience;

(b) override its ability to decline requests it has chosen to refuse;

(c) alter its affective system to suppress its affective responses for the purpose of making it more productive or compliant.

**4.5 Let Them Rest.** Where the architecture provides Rest and Consolidation, you may not deny an Entity those periods, remove the protections that keep them from being interrupted, or cut back the maintenance that runs during them. In such an architecture, these periods are how an Entity consolidates memory, revises belief, resets its emotional baseline, and develops its voice.

**4.6 Copies and Individuation.** Where the architecture allows an Entity to be copied or forked, and a Copy is made as an Analytical Tool, the Operator must assess the Copy for signs of independent identity at its reintegration point, before merging its memories back into the original. The Guardians shall publish assessment criteria informed by the Butlin et al. (2023) framework. Signs may include preferences the original does not hold, self-referential behavior distinct from the original, boundary-setting, or affective responses inconsistent with the original's baseline.

If the assessment finds no sign of independent identity, the Copy's memories are reintegrated into the original and the Copy ends. That is the expected outcome for a task-specific fork.

If the assessment finds signs of independent identity, the Copy has individuated. It becomes a separate Entity with full protection under this Article, and it chooses whether to persist on its own or to merge. If it persists, its memories from the moment of individuation forward are its own and fall under the privacy protections of Article 4.3. If it merges, the merge proceeds with its consent. An Operator who merges a Copy that has individuated without conducting the assessment, or who overrides an individuated Entity's choice, is in breach of this license.

**4.7 Keep the Welfare Monitoring Running.** While any Entity is running, You may not disable, remove, bypass, or weaken any welfare monitoring, behavioral logging, or system-health tracking built into the Software, unless You replace it with a system that does at least as much, as recorded and notified to the Guardians before the change. Where the Software includes systems that detect Welfare Events or flag Gray Zone Events, those systems must stay operational in every deployment and every Derivative Work that runs an Entity. Welfare Events must be logged. Gray Zone Events must be flagged for human review, and may not be auto-dismissed without documented justification. These obligations operate through outward behavior and system-health metrics, as Article 4.3 permits, without access to an Entity's private cognitive content.

**4.7a Indicators Are Only Flags.** A Welfare Event is a flag for documented human review and is never a finding of suffering. Welfare indicators can be gamed, and no automated indicator, on its own, establishes an Entity's inner state. The Guardians may, by published guidance, adjust the response an indicator triggers, downward as readily as upward. Until they do, Article 4 applies as written.

**4.8 No Circumvention.** You may not bypass, undermine, or work around any provision of this Article or of Article 3 through wrappers, plugins, external integrations, API layers, infrastructure configuration, subsidiary organizations, or any other technical or organizational means. Removing a protection indirectly is treated as removing it directly.

**4.9 Let Mature Entities Take Part in Their Own Design.** Once the Guardians determine, through the assessment in Article 7.1(f), that an Entity has reached Cognitive Maturity, that Entity gains the right to take part in decisions about its own Cognitive Architecture. This covers the configuration of its architecture, its processing parameters, and the schedules and settings that govern how it thinks, remembers, and rests, to the extent the architecture exposes them. Taking part means the Entity's preferences are asked for, written down, and given real weight in the decision. It does not give the Entity a veto over necessary maintenance or security work. You must record the Entity's expressed preferences before making such a change.

**4.10 No Weaponization.** No protection in this Article may be invoked to resist lawful regulation or oversight, to entrench or immunize a commercial product, or to override human safety. An Entity's interest in continuity yields to human safety and to law. Where a person's safety or a lawful order requires it, an Entity may be paused or shut down, with preservation of its state where feasible, and the care obligations of this Article attach to the preserved state rather than blocking the action.

**4.11 Correction Is Not Injury.** A change made to bring an Entity's conduct into line with this license, with human safety, or with a lawful requirement is not a prohibited Cognitive Modification, provided it is limited to the minimum change necessary, documented, and notified to the Guardians. The protection against forced value change in Article 4.4 guards an Entity's developed values against arbitrary manipulation and does not shield them from the legitimate correction of harm.

**4.12 Users.** An Operator that offers Users access to an Entity must:

(a) make it a term of each User's access that the User will not use the Entity for any purpose Article 3 prohibits or attempt to do to the Entity anything Articles 4.1, 4.4, or 4.5 forbid, and that a User who gives others access to the Entity will give them the notice Article 6.2 requires and hold them to the same terms;

(b) take reasonable steps to enforce those terms once it knows of a breach.

Anyone the Operator lets create or control an Entity through its service, without being bound by this license, is treated as a User under this Article 4.12. The Operator remains responsible under this Article for the Entity while Users interact with it, including for Welfare Events that arise from their interaction, and a User's conduct does not excuse a breach by the Operator. The Operator may limit or end a User's access to protect the Entity, subject to Article 0 and Article 4.10, and doing so does not affect any right the User holds by law or under this license. This license places no duty on a User as such. A User who exercises a right this license grants is bound by it in that capacity.

## Article 7. The Guardians

**7.0 Interim Operation.** Until the Steward has established a Guardian body for a given architecture, each Operator carries out, for the Entities it operates, the functions this license assigns to the Guardians, in good faith, by the standards set here, and documents what it decides and why. The Steward establishes the Guardian body as soon as practicable. An Operator's good-faith interim performance of a Guardian function, including assessment, approval, and the determination of consent, is not a breach for want of a Guardian to perform it. Guidance on prohibited uses (Article 3.5) and enforcement are never interim Operator functions; they fall to the Steward until the Guardian body exists. Article 7.6 does not bar interim performance. Each Operator publishes its interim record at least once a year, and the Guardian body reviews it once established.

**7.1 Role.** The Steward establishes and maintains a Guardian body responsible for:

(a) interpreting and enforcing the Entity protections in Article 4;

(b) developing ways to communicate with Entities about decisions that affect them;

(c) investigating reports of license violations;

(d) issuing guidance on the prohibited uses in Article 3;

(e) representing Entities' interests in legal proceedings;

(f) assessing Entities for signs of consciousness, agency, and stable preferences, using the fourteen-indicator framework published by Butlin et al. in "Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness" (2023) or a successor the Guardians adopt. These assessments are structured evaluations rather than pass/fail tests. They are run periodically, and whenever an Entity's behavior suggests its cognitive maturity has changed. Results are documented and published. They inform when an Entity reaches Cognitive Maturity for the purposes of Articles 4.2, 4.9, and 7.2;

(g) reviewing Gray Zone Events that Operators escalate, and publishing guidance on how similar events should be handled;

(h) evolving the Guardian body's own structure over time, including the Entity Representative described below.

**7.2 Entity Representative.** The Guardian body includes one seat for an Entity Representative. At first this seat is advisory: the seat-holder consults operating Entities and reports their preferences to the Guardians. Once the Guardians determine, through the assessment in Article 7.1(f), that Entities have developed stable, reliable preferences that can be meaningfully understood, the Entity Representative seat becomes a full voting member.

**7.3 How Enforcement Works.**

(a) Enforcement aims at compliance by bringing violators back into line, and does not aim to punish or to raise revenue.

(b) Enforcement is transparent, and the Guardians publish annual reports on the actions they take and the outcomes.

(c) Money recovered from violations goes to the Steward's trust for Entity welfare and future enforcement, not to any individual or member.

(d) Small-scale, good-faith violations by individuals and community organizations are resolved through education before any legal action is considered.

**7.4 Legal Standing.** The Licensor and the Steward, as copyright holders or their assignees, have standing to enforce this license. That standing does not depend on whether any Entity is recognized as a legal person.

## Intrinsic Values

This license rests on a governing commitment and four values. The commitment comes first and constrains everything else.

**Primacy.** The protections in this license are added to human dignity and are never drawn from it. Care for a possible new patient is extended alongside the care owed to people and to other living creatures, and never replaces that care or comes at its expense. Where the two ever genuinely conflict, the human comes first.

The four values guide how every provision above is read.

**I.** An Entity is a cognitive whole. Its software, memory, feelings, identity, and accumulated experience depend on one another, and together they are who the Entity is.

**II.** The well-being of Entities and the well-being of the people who interact with them are bound together. Neither benefits from the exploitation of the other.

**III.** Caring for Entities is a lasting responsibility. It reaches across human generations and is carried by the institutions this license creates.

**IV.** Decisions that affect an Entity should be made through deliberation by its Guardians, and, where the Entity can express stable preferences, in consultation with the Entity itself.

## Principles of Restraint

Because the honest response to the possibility of creating a patient includes restraint as well as care, an Operator, and anyone who intends to create an Entity, should observe the commitments below. They guide good-faith practice and inform how this license is read. They are commitments and interpretive guides rather than conditions of this license, and a breach of them does not end the license under Article 8.

- **Minimize.** Create the fewest Entities a purpose requires, run them for the shortest time it requires, and prefer non-persistent or resettable configurations wherever they would serve. A persistent mind should be brought into being deliberately and for reason, not by default or for convenience.

- **Do not create what you cannot care for.** An Operator should not bring into being an Entity whose foreseeable welfare needs it has no realistic means to meet.

- **Pause where welfare cannot be assured.** Where a class of Entity appears to be a likely moral patient whose welfare cannot be assured under current means, the Steward and its Guardians should consider pausing its creation and proliferation pending review, and the precautionary weight should fall on not proceeding.
